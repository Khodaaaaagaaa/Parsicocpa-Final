Missing 1095-A in 2026 – Marketplace Statements and Premium Tax Credit Reconciliation

PT
Parsi Team Content Group — Reviewed by Parsi Team, CPA
October 14, 2026
Last Reviewed: October 14, 2026
6 min read
Prepared by Parsi Team

This article was drafted by the Parsi Team content group and subjected to technical, legal, and compliance review and final approval by Parsi Team, CPA, prior to publication.

A missing 1095-A can leave the premium tax credit calculation unresolved on an individual return. Form 1095-A, Health Insurance Marketplace Statement, supplies coverage and financial-assistance information used in preparing Form 8962. It serves a different purpose from an insurance card or a record of monthly bank payments.

This educational publication addresses Marketplace coverage during 2025 and individual returns prepared in 2026. It explains document recovery, reconciliation and California considerations. It does not determine a household's eligibility, credit, repayment amount or coverage exemption.

What This Article Covers
  • Form 1095-A compared with other health-coverage documents.
  • Locating a missing Marketplace statement.
  • Advance premium tax credit and the final reconciliation.
  • Multiple, corrected and voided statements.
  • California coverage reporting and unresolved filing issues.

1. Form 1095-A Compared with Other Health-Coverage Documents

Form 1095-A relates to Marketplace coverage. Form 1095-B reports health coverage, while Form 1095-C concerns employer-provided health insurance offers and coverage. These forms are not interchangeable. IRS guidance distinguishes waiting for an expected 1095-A from the general ability to file a federal return without waiting for Forms 1095-B or 1095-C.

The distinction matters for a household that changed coverage during the year. An employer statement may document one period while a Marketplace statement documents another. Having one does not establish that the other is unnecessary.

DocumentInformation it can provideUnresolved question

A card can help identify the plan involved, and payment records can provide context for a discrepancy. Neither supplies the same reporting package as a Marketplace statement.

2. Locating a Missing Marketplace Statement

For California Marketplace enrollees, Covered California describes delivery by mail or through the account's online mailbox. Its official tax-form page also explains its process for disputed information and corrected forms.

An unavailable statement can reflect a delivery issue, an outdated address or a misunderstanding about which account contains the document. The preparation file can distinguish a statement that has not been located from one whose information is being disputed.

Relevant identifying records commonly include the Marketplace case information, policy details, coverage period and any correspondence concerning the tax form. These records help connect the request to the correct plan and year.

The distinction between a missing form and an unexpected form also matters. Not every type of health coverage generates a 1095-A. A household's coverage history provides the starting point for identifying which documents are expected.

A hypothetical taxpayer has an employer insurance card for the second half of the year and recalls Marketplace coverage earlier. The employer card does not resolve the earlier coverage period. A month-by-month history makes the missing statement easier to identify without assuming full-year coverage under one plan.

3. Advance Premium Tax Credit and the Final Reconciliation

Advance premium tax credit, often abbreviated APTC, is assistance paid during the year to the insurer. Form 8962 compares those advance payments with the premium tax credit calculated for the return. If APTC was paid for a person in the tax family, the instructions generally require an income tax return with Form 8962 even when a return would not otherwise be required.

The amount estimated during enrollment and the amount determined on the return can differ. The final calculation depends on the applicable eligibility and household information. Receiving assistance during the year does not establish the final credit amount.

The return-preparation file can separately identify the Marketplace's figures and the household information used in the tax calculation. That separation makes it easier to understand whether an unresolved issue concerns the statement, the household facts or both.

This article concerns 2025 coverage. Eligibility and repayment rules for another tax year cannot be assumed to be identical merely because the form number remains the same.

⚠ Important Note

The IRS explains that omission of a required Form 8962 can delay return processing and affect future advance-credit eligibility. The general federal extended filing deadline for a timely extension is October 15, 2026, subject to special relief. A missing statement does not itself provide another extension.

4. Multiple, Corrected and Voided Statements

A household may receive more than one 1095-A after plan changes or when more than one policy covered household members. The document inventory can identify policy numbers, covered individuals, coverage periods and document versions.

A second statement is therefore not automatically a duplicate. It may describe another policy or replace an earlier statement. Treating every later document as additional coverage can be as misleading as treating every later document as a replacement.

For multiple policies, Form 8962 has specific calculation rules. In particular, the applicable second-lowest-cost Silver plan premium is not simply the sum of every benchmark amount printed on every form. The instructions govern the treatment.

The IRS distinguishes corrected forms from voided forms. A corrected statement can change the calculation; a voided statement indicates that the affected form is not used for reporting. If a return has already been filed, the effect of the new document and any correspondence requires separate analysis.

Clear version labels are useful here. A file containing “original,” “corrected” and “voided” documents without identifying which policy each concerns can leave the reconciliation ambiguous.

5. California Coverage Reporting and Unresolved Filing Issues

California's individual health-care mandate is separate from federal premium tax credit reconciliation. California residents generally need qualifying coverage, an applicable exemption or a state penalty determination. FTB Form 3853 addresses exemptions and the individual shared responsibility penalty.

A federal credit calculation does not establish that all state coverage questions are resolved. A month-by-month coverage history may be relevant to both reviews, but each review has its own purpose.

For Los Angeles households with employer and Marketplace coverage during different periods, that history can identify gaps or overlapping records without presuming a penalty applies. Exemptions and other rules can affect the state result.

The broader filing context appears in Tax Extensions 2026 and the October 15 individual filing checklist. A separate discussion of payment timing appears in Quarterly Estimated Taxes 2026.

A missing statement remains a documentation issue; an unresolved credit is a calculation issue. Identifying the distinction helps explain what information remains outstanding without substituting this general publication for a filing determination.

Key Takeaways
  • Form 1095-A serves a different purpose from Forms 1095-B and 1095-C.
  • Marketplace account records can help locate an unavailable statement.
  • Advance assistance and the final premium tax credit can differ.
  • Multiple policies and document versions require separate identification.
  • California coverage reporting is distinct from federal credit reconciliation.
